About Me
The writer you're about to hire!
I'm a technical writer who specializes in making complex technology understandable for everyone. With a background in IT support, publishing, and journalism, I excel at creating clear, user-focused documentation. I reduce customer frustration and give users more control over the tools they use. Whether it's API guides, tutorials, or prompt engineering, I translate "technobabble" into plain English.
My first job was as a media services technician at the New York City College of Technology, where I studied at the time. Guiding non-technical users through troubleshooting started my technical writing career.
I view my role as that of a translator, a skill I've honed since childhood. As the child of an immigrant family, I was often the designated translator for English and legalese. I still use that skill today, bridging the gap between complex technology and the people who use it.

Content Patterns
Design standards governing these portfolio studies. Each pattern documents trigger conditions, required voice, and copy structure.
Error states
State the cause in the first clause, before giving the fix.
Put the recovery step second, as an imperative: "Re-enter the email you signed up with."
Never blame the reader. The fake-name study shows the anti-pattern, then the rewrite.
Enforce character limits of 45 for headlines and 100 for body copy, while capping button labels at 25.
Drop exclamation marks on failure. Calm copy reads as competence.
Alert copy
Lead with the consequence and the timeframe, then the action.
Provide one action per alert. Competing choices create decision fatigue during emergencies.
Escalate by severity, not repetition. Capitalized keywords communicate urgency directly.
State the user's next action instead of reporting internal system status.
Subscription & meta copy
Put the benefit in the headline. Put the price in the body.
Cut urgency adjectives. "Ends Friday" beats "last chance."
Write the meta description as a standalone sentence, because it renders without the page.
Keep the button label to the outcome, not the mechanics.
Case files outline design constraints, copy rationale, and final UI strings. The featured hall passes apply this same discipline to print media.
