UX Writing & Content Design

Screen copy, error states, and notification copy written to real character limits, each published as an interactive Figma prototype.

Each study isolates a specific user drop-off point and supplies constraint-driven screen copy. These 18 portfolio projects demonstrate systematic UX content design. Content Patterns below outlines the underlying rules.

Banking App Onboarding Flow

User Problem & Context: New banking app users feel anxious and abandon multi-step financial setup. Guide them through account creation, bill categorization, and automatic monthly payments while building trust in security.

Constraints
headline: 45 characters
body: 100 characters
buttons: 25 characters

Strategy & Rationale

The strategy builds trust at each step. Plain, calm copy makes bill automation feel safe. Each screen shows one benefit so users aren't overwhelmed.

  • Screen 1: The Core Value Proposition. The first screen addresses convenience right away with the headline Never fear, Paymate's here! and establishes the primary benefit: automated bills and timely reminders.
  • Screen 2: Building Security Trust. Before asking for user data, the second screen addresses user security concerns directly. The headline Fort Knox security. In your pocket. uses a familiar metaphor to convey safety, while the body copy lists the safeguards that protect the account.
  • Screen 3: Highlighting Smart Features. This screen introduces automated bill categorization. The headline Smartphone, smart banking, smart user reinforces user agency and highlights system intelligence.
  • Screens 4-7: The Setup Process. After the first screens build trust, the flow moves to account creation and payment setup. Concise microcopy (Enough about us, let's focus on you.) guides the user through manageable forms, culminating in a clear success confirmation (Welcome Aboard!).

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Progressive disclosure reduces anxiety one step at a time, which cuts setup friction and lifts completion rates. The flow turns a complex financial signup into a high-trust, low-friction journey.

App Error with Unknown Cause

User Problem & Context: A system outage blocks access to price comparison data and returns no error code. Tell the user plainly what happened, prevent churn, and offer recovery options right away.

Constraints
headline: 30 characters max
body: 120 characters max
buttons: 15 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

This case study shows how a voice gets calibrated and why a first draft can fail. When users lose the app's core function, humor feels dismissive. The design moves from flippant fiction to direct, transparent, empathetic recovery.

  • Draft 1 (Scrapped, First-Draft Failure).
    Headline: ERROR CODE: 42 (14 chars) • Body: "It looks like the elf we have tracking your prices (Alan) stepped out for a quick call or something. Let's give Al a second. If you're in a rush, try our website." (162 chars) • Buttons: RETRY, WEBSITE Why it failed: flippant humor frustrates users during an outage. The whimsical narrative delays recovery and runs past the 120-character limit. The invented error code also hides the root cause.
  • Draft 2 (Approved, Calibrated Copy).
    Headline: Prices are unavailable. (23 chars) • Body: "We can't load prices right now. Please try again soon or visit our website for the latest info and prices." (106 chars) • Buttons: RETRY, WEBSITE Why it succeeded: complete transparency states the system constraint directly without technical jargon. Clear dual-path recovery (RETRY versus WEBSITE) preserves user agency and protects brand credibility.
  • Content Strategy Takeaway.
    Strong content design requires knowing when not to use humor. Critical error recovery demands total clarity, empathy, and rapid recovery paths over playful branding.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Clear copy protects the brand during an outage. When users can recover on their own, support tickets drop and engagement holds.

Flight Cancellation Notification

User Problem & Context: Severe weather cancels a traveler's flight without warning. Alert the passenger immediately with directions to the rebooking desk, written so they can act without panic.

Constraints
headline: 45 characters
body: 175 characters max
buttons: 25 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

A flight disruption needs a direct voice. Fast comprehension beats small talk.

  • Headline: FLIGHT CANCELLED. GO TO HELP DESK delivers maximum scannability, answering what happened and where to go in one glance.
  • Body: The copy gives the specific flight (DOC063) and the cause (“inclement weather”). It reinforces direct action: “Please head to the help desk.”
  • Buttons: The Got it button dismisses the alert, while Airport Map opens wayfinding so travelers can orient themselves inside large terminals.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Plain wording calms the traveler and routes the crowd toward the service counters. Fast mobile wayfinding reduces distress and protects long-term brand goodwill during severe disruptions.

Fire Alert Notification

User Problem & Context: An active wildfire near a driver's commute threatens road closures and personal safety. Deliver an urgent notification that reads in one glance and prompts immediate, safe rerouting.

Constraints
headline: 30 characters max
body: 45 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

Safety alerts that reach drivers must read in one glance.

  • Design and Tone: High-contrast warning colors and concise all-caps typography communicate urgent safety priority without cluttering the screen.
  • Headline: FIRE ON ATLANTIC AVE communicates danger and geographic proximity in under 25 characters.
  • Body: FIRE MAY AFFECT YOUR ROUTE. REROUTE. instructs action without diverting attention from driving.
  • Button: REROUTE TO SAFETY makes the action decisive and safety-focused.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Putting driver safety first keeps users out of danger zones and meets the platform's duty to protect them.

Expired Credit Card Error

User Problem & Context: A customer tries to finish a time-sensitive car rental reservation, but the saved card has expired. Point to the exact problem and send the user to update the details without losing the booking.

Constraints
headline: 30 characters
body: 45 characters

Strategy & Rationale

Checkout payment errors need a direct, clear way out. Ambiguity here loses the sale.

  • Headline: YOUR CARD IS EXPIRED! gives the exact breakdown instantly, so users don't mistake it for a credit limit, a network drop, or expired credentials.
  • Body: Update card details or add a new one gives clear instructions for clearing the payment obstacle right away.
  • UI Alignment: Visual form cues highlight the expiration date field, which pulls the eye straight to the fix.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Clear copy about a failed payment saves the sale.

“Fake Name” Error Message

User Problem & Context: Automated fraud detection flags a legitimate user's unique or uncommon name during account creation. Prompt identity verification respectfully without alienating or shaming the user.

Constraints
message: 45 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

Automated fraud checks often mark unusual and culturally diverse names as risky. The copy turns that doubt into a polite request for proof.

  • Inclusive, Affirming Tone: Rather than issuing a negative error code, That's an interesting name! turns an automated rejection into a courteous human exchange.
  • Collaborative Microcopy: Please help us verify it invites the user to help instead of issuing an accusation.
  • Direct Verification Pathway: An embedded verification link confirms identity right away without forcing the user out of the flow.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Empathy in an error message keeps diverse customers on board and protects lifetime value.

Contact Lens Subscription - Title & Meta Description

User Problem & Context: Visually impaired and elderly users experience tech anxiety when considering recurring medical deliveries. Craft search-optimized title and meta copy that builds trust and emphasizes effortless, doorstep replenishment.

Constraints
title: 65 characters max
meta description: 160 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

Accessible health care needs plain words and large, clear text for older readers.

  • Title: VisionQuest: Hassle-Free Contact Lens Delivery Every 30 Days! emphasizes reliability and a predictable service cadence.
  • Meta Description: The description puts a number on setup time (“15 minutes or less”) and leads with doorstep delivery, which lowers the effort users expect.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Clear, accessible metadata brings qualified clicks from careful buyers and grows subscriptions.

Truck Driver Delay Notification

User Problem & Context: A short-haul driver runs late because of highway traffic. Deliver a push notification that reads in one glance and offers fast routing choices without pulling attention off the road.

Constraints
headline: 30 characters max
body: 45 characters max
buttons: 25 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

Logistics notifications must stay short so drivers keep their attention on the road.

  • Non-Accusatory Tone: The headline Running Late? assigns no blame, so a sync lag or a traffic jam fits either way.
  • Extreme Brevity: Short phrasing reads cleanly through audio screen assistants.
  • Actionable Choices: Fuel up now or keep going? pairs with two distinct buttons (REROUTE TO PITSTOP and KEEP GOING). The choice takes one tap.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Timely notifications keep deliveries on schedule and protect driver welfare.

Car Buying Website Location Request

User Problem & Context: A vehicle marketplace needs the user's location to show local inventory and regional pricing. Ask for it transparently so privacy doubts don't cost the sale.

Constraints
headline: 25 characters
body: 45 characters
button: 15 characters

Strategy & Rationale

Users grant permission when the interface ties data collection to a clear benefit.

  • Benefit-Oriented Headline: Your dream car is here. leads with the user's goal, not the company's data need.
  • Engaging Personality: Playful microcopy (Yes, even THAT one) builds a friendly brand voice.
  • Privacy-Conscious Input: Asking for a ZIP code instead of a street address respects privacy and still fills regional inventory.
  • Compelling CTA: VIEW OUR DEALS frames data entry as unlocking discounts instead of handing over personal details.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Clear value framing raises how many users say yes and moves them into browsing.

App Crash Recovery Message

User Problem & Context: An unexpected shutdown interrupts a graphic designer mid-critique. Reassure the user at restart, confirm whether recovery worked, and offer plain paths to restore or start fresh.

Constraints
headline: 40 characters max
body: 140 characters max
buttons: 20 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

System failures cause acute anxiety about lost work. The restart dialog has to confirm recovery at once and get the user back to work.

  • Headline: ACME DESIGNS Crashed Unexpectedly names the failure without excuses.
  • Body: Your design files were recovered immediately confirms recovery before offering workspace options.
  • Buttons: Restore and New give two clear actions.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

A clear crash message keeps user trust and cuts app abandonment.

Incorrect Email Error Message

User Problem & Context: A returning user types an email that's mistyped or not on file. State the specific sign-in problem and echo the input so the typo shows right away without revealing whether an account exists.

Constraints
message: 40 characters max

Strategy & Rationale

Sign-in friction is a top drop-off point. An explicit error message moves users straight to the fix.

  • Targeted Clarity: Retype email address, '[email]' tells the user exactly where the mismatch is and echoes their entry so the typo is visible right away.
  • Direct Action Verb: Leading with the imperative “Retype” names the next step without ambiguity.

Business Impact & Intended Outcome

Targeted inline feedback cuts password reset requests and unblocks sign-in fast.

Content Patterns

Design standards governing these portfolio studies. Each pattern documents trigger conditions, required voice, and copy structure.

Error states 5

  1. State the cause in the first clause, before giving the fix.
  2. Put the recovery step second, as an imperative: “Re-enter the email you signed up with.”
  3. Never blame the reader. The fake-name study shows the anti-pattern, then the rewrite.
  4. Enforce character limits of 45 for headlines and 100 for body copy, while capping button labels at 25.
  5. Drop exclamation marks on failure. Calm copy reads as competence.

Alert copy 3

  1. Lead with the consequence and the timeframe, then the action.
  2. Provide one action per alert. Competing choices create decision fatigue during emergencies.
  3. Escalate by severity, not repetition. Capitalized keywords communicate urgency directly.
  4. State the user's next action instead of reporting internal system status.

Subscription & meta copy 5

  1. Put the benefit in the headline. Put the price in the body.
  2. Cut urgency adjectives. “Ends Friday” beats “last chance.”
  3. Write the meta description as a standalone sentence, because it renders without the page.
  4. Keep the button label to the outcome, not the mechanics.
